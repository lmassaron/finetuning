Ventricular septal defect (Epidemiology) : VSDs are the most common congenital cardiac abnormalities. They are found in 30–60% of all newborns with a congenital heart defect, or about 2–6 per 1000 births. During heart formation, when the heart begins life as a hollow tube, it begins to partition, forming septa. If this does not occur properly it can lead to an opening being left within the ventricular septum. It is debatable whether all those defects are true heart defects, or if some of them are normal phenomena, since most of the trabecular VSDs close spontaneously. Prospective studies give a prevalence of 2–5 per 100 births of trabecular VSDs that close shortly after birth in 80–90% of the cases.
