Dilated cardiomyopathy (Epidemiology) : Although the disease is more common in African-Americans than in Caucasians, it may occur in any patient population. Approximately 6 cases occur a year for every 1,000 people in the U.S. . The age adjusted mortality rate (AAMR) has declined over the last two decades from 5.19 deaths to 1.98 deaths per 100,000. Because the AAMR has decreased, this suggests that the overall death rate after accounting for age, has a net negative effect. Though, during the COVID-19 pandemic, the AAMR had increased to 2.34, suggesting the presence of the pandemic likely had an impact on it. Sex wise, men are at a significantly higher risk compared to women. Significant trends show when measuring the age of effected individuals. African Americans tend to show signs by the age of 49 while Caucasian men showed at age 55.
