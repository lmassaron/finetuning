Atrial fibrillation (Other animals) : Atrial fibrillation occurs in other animals, including cats, dogs, and horses. Unlike humans, dogs rarely develop the complications that stem from blood clots breaking off from inside the heart and traveling through the arteries to distant sites (thromboembolic complications). Cats rarely develop atrial fibrillation but appear to have a higher risk of thromboembolic complications than dogs. Cats and dogs with atrial fibrillation often have underlying structural heart disease that predisposes them to the condition. The medications used in animals for atrial fibrillation are largely similar to those used in humans. Electrical cardioversion is occasionally performed in these animals, but the need for general anesthesia limits its use. Standardbred horses appear to be genetically susceptible to developing atrial fibrillation. Horses that develop atrial fibrillation often have minimal or no underlying heart disease, and the presence of atrial fibrillation in horses can adversely affect physical performance.
