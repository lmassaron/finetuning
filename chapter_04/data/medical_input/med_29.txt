Angina (Prognosis) : Angina may persist or recur after ballon angioplasty, a phenomenon called post-percutaneous intervention (PCI) angina. The prevalence of post-PCI angina is widely recognized to affect between 20% and 40% of patients during short-to-medium-term follow-up (Three months to One year). Other studies suggest that between 20% to 60% of patients continue to experience anginal symptoms after their procedure. The pathophysiological landscape of post-PCI angina is multifactorial, involving a complex interplay between mechanical, functional, and patient-specific factors. These mechanisms are generally categorized into obstructive causes, such as incomplete revascularization (where flow-limiting lesions are left unaddressed) or stent failure (including restenosis and thrombosis), and non-obstructive causes, such as coronary microvascular dysfunction and vasomotor disorders like epicardial or microvascular spasms.
